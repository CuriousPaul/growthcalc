About these models

Two classic growth-hacking models for projecting how an app's user base evolves over time. Plug in your acquisition channels, your virality, and your retention — see how they interact.

Simple Loss

Each month a flat percentage of the existing user base is lost. Virality is modeled as viral[m] = gross_growth[m-1] × k, i.e. last month's new users invite their friends. Useful for a quick back-of-envelope, but real retention is rarely a flat percentage.

Retention & Virality Curves

Cohort-based. Each acquisition cohort retains along the configured retention curve, and each cohort generates virality that decays month-over-month. New users at the start of each month are the sum of every prior cohort weighted by retention: 

users_start[m+1] = Σ cohort[c] × retention[m+1-c]
viral[m]         = Σ cohort[c] × retention[m-c] × viral_factor[m-c]

Months 7+ use the month 6 retention as a steady-state floor. Viral factor decays geometrically past month 6.

Viral Coefficient

Per-cycle exponential model. K = participation × i × c, where i is the average shares per active sharer and c is the conversion rate per share. New subscribers in cycle n are seed × Kⁿ; cumulative subscribers after N cycles are seed × (K^(N+1) − 1) / (K − 1).

  • K < 1: growth decays. Theoretical max reach with infinite time is seed / (1 − K).
  • K = 1: each cycle adds the same number of users (linear growth, no ceiling).
  • K > 1: pure exponential. In reality saturation and decay kick in; layer this on top of retention curves above for a realistic projection.
